- **Ice axe climb — shipped 9/21**: the real "ice climb" payoff past the breather stretch — a tall jagged ice wall with 9 alternating handholds leading up to a landing ledge. Deliberately built as a genuinely new third climb mechanic (distinct from the fungus tree's rhythm-bounce and the forest rock ledge's grab-then-fixed-arc): grab a hold, then release (Up) to launch in a calibrated arc to the next one, or drop (Down) to bail back down — reusing the forest rock climb's proven arc math and handhold spacing wholesale, right down to the exact tilt/speed numbers, so the hop-to-hop feel is the same precision "climby not bouncy" arc players already know. The new hook: **grip decay** — a held ice hold doesn't hold forever. Its own crack visibly radiates outward and widens the longer you cling to it (a pure function of hold time, same "no persisted animation state" approach as everything else ambient in this game), and if you outstay it, it lets go on its own and slips you back ~2 holds with a quick ice-chip burst — rather than a hard reset to the bottom. Reaching the top ledge (either by a real in-flight release or by walking straight onto it) plants you cleanly, no reset. Framed as the "climb up (tension)" half of a future "climb up, then snowboard down (release)" arc — the snowboard-down payoff is intentionally deferred as its own later build. Built and verified via a full 8-hop chain-climb test (every hop lands exactly on its target hold) plus dedicated tests for the grip-decay slip (both mid-climb and near the bottom) and the top-ledge landing from an in-flight release. One real bug caught and fixed along the way: the top-ledge landing off a launched release wasn't being caught by the generic landing code (which the launched-flight physics path never falls through to) and had to get its own dedicated in-flight catch, mirroring an identical historical fix already in place for the forest rock ledge. The ice-chip slip-burst color was also punched up after testing showed the original was too close to the wall/sky's own pale blue to read clearly. **Follow-up same day ("this is literally the exact same as rock climb to pool but white")**: direct, blunt feedback that a first pass read as a reskin, not a new set-piece — fair, since the grip-decay window (1700ms) was loose enough that confident normal play never actually felt it. Pushed significantly further on both the visual and mechanical front per direct follow-up ("i also like 2, 3. and yes 1 too" / "i want ice pick animation, at absolute minimum" / "this needs to be significantly different an legit"): (1) grip windows tightened hard and now vary per hold (1000ms strong / 550ms weak) so decay is a constant felt pressure, not an edge case; (2) roughly a third of the wall is now pre-cracked/WEAK holds, visibly so (a real static fracture mark) before you ever grab them, and never the very first hold — a genuine route-read layer rock climb has no equivalent of; (3) every hop is now steerable (a first for a climb set-piece — rock climb's arc is fixed) AND pushed by a real mid-air wind gust, so each hop is an active aim-and-correct skill moment instead of "press up, trust the arc" — verified fair via a full real-time 8-hop chain test (all land cleanly even with wind); (4) a real ice-axe swing animation, the explicit minimum ask — the axe now belongs to the PLAYER, not the wall: it winds up and strikes in on every grab (small impact flecks on landing), rests planted while held, and yanks free on release or a grip-decay slip, instead of every hold showing an axe already sitting there embedded and static. Hit one real rendering bug getting the swing to actually show: the axe was anchored at the player's own foot-level point, so the player's own sprite (drawn on top, later in the frame) covered almost all of it — fixed by anchoring near shoulder/head height instead, offset toward the hold's push side, so it clears the sprite in every pose. Also found mid-debugging that the debug harness's default starting inventory (a shovel) was rendering its own held-item icon in the exact same spot and had been the thing I was actually looking at through several rounds of "did my change do anything" confusion — not a game bug, just a misleading test fixture. **Second follow-up round, same day ("i dont really see the axe swing. also it is v v v easy to just keep clicking uparrow and go up the whole thing quickly the exact same as rock climb to pool")**: the anchor-occlusion fix above made the axe visible in a zoomed screenshot, but at real gameplay scale/speed it still wasn't landing, and — the bigger issue — nothing stopped a player from just mashing Up through the whole wall, skipping the swing and the grip-decay pressure entirely. Fixed with one real input gate: `winterIceClimbRelease()` can no longer fire on Up until the axe's own strike animation has actually finished landing on that hold (currently 380ms, tuned up from an initial 220ms once testing showed 220 still read as barely-there) — an early Up press is simply ignored, not queued, so you physically cannot climb faster than the swing plays. Verified directly against the real `keys.upJustPressed` input path (not just the debug harness's `releaseIceHold()` shortcut, which bypasses this gate): rapid-fire Up presses right after a grab are confirmed no-ops while the strike is still playing, and release only goes through once the gate clears. **Third follow-up, same thread ("i want like a thing that looks like organic ice ledges, and ice cracks, that the axe actually goes into. with a little more animation on that")**: replaced the flat white ellipse "grip knob" with an irregular, jagged ice-ledge shape (per-hold seeded, stretched toward the direction the hold pushes off, with a top-lit facet) that carries real crack texture baked directly into its surface — faint on a strong hold, visibly worse on a weak one before you ever touch it — all fanning from one shared strike point that the axe itself now physically plants into (previously the axe and the hold's crack weren't tied to the same point). The axe's own swing got bigger too: a wider wind-up arc, a higher raise, and on strike, both an expanding shock ring and a bigger fleck burst instead of just four small dots. Grip-decay cracks now also get a slow pulse while held so they don't just sit static once they've grown. One side effect worth noting: since the swing-lock above eats 380ms of every hold non-negotiable, the original weak-hold grip window (550ms) would've left almost no real reactive time after the strike lands — bumped to 680ms so a weak hold still reads as meaningfully tighter than a strong one (1000ms) without being unfair. Re-verified the full 8-hop chain climb lands exactly on every hold with all the new numbers, with no regressions. **Fourth follow-up, same thread -- a real rebuild ("maybe not have the just back and forth only left and right going up. like, maybe there are a few different paths. the rock wall is a lot wider. and some paths are just not feasable, but you can only see that too much before you actually try it" + "like some weak, some shallow, holds" + "not always obvious they are good, or bad. could be strong smaller holds too" + "it drops you down a little, like 1-3 levels depending what and where holds are below you... doesnt have to be directly down but close ish. and if none really close directly down you just fall further down until there is one within some width")**: the single-file zigzag climb (one hold per height step, strictly alternating sides) is gone. The wall is now genuinely wide (edge geometry roughly doubled) with 2-3 real candidate holds per height band ("row") instead of one, so there's an actual route to read and choose between rather than a fixed sequence with a timer bolted on. Three hold qualities now exist: strong (secure), weak (visibly pre-cracked before you ever touch it, crumbles if you linger, no hard stopwatch feel), and a brand new shallow -- no visible tell, looks like it could be anything, and ALWAYS crumbles shortly after you grab it (470ms, timed to land just after the axe's own strike finishes) no matter how fast you react, because it was never a real hold to begin with. Visual size leans toward matching quality but is a deliberately unreliable hint -- about 1 in 5 holds subvert it on purpose, so a small hold being rock solid (or a big one being shallow) is a real, seeded possibility, not just flavor text. Falling (whether from a weak hold's timeout or a shallow one's guaranteed crumble) now runs a real downward search instead of a scripted "go back exactly 2 holds": it looks at everything below you, starts with a narrow horizontal tolerance and widens it (falling further) until something's in reach, then picks among up to the 3 nearest-by-height candidates weighted toward closer but genuinely not always the closest. Launches are no longer aimed via a fixed per-hold direction either -- since a hold's next-row options can now be left, right, or center, baking in a bias would fight whichever way you actually want to go, so releases now start neutral and horizontal placement comes entirely from steering + the existing wind gust. Caught one real bug from that change during testing: with zero baseline horizontal push, the player could end up sitting close enough (in both x and y) to the hold they'd JUST released from that the game's own passive mid-air catch would immediately re-grab it, silently eating the release. Fixed by tracking which hold a flight actually departed from and excluding it from both mid-air catch checks for the whole flight. Verified with real Playwright-driven climbs (actual steering key input, polling the real release gate instead of guessing fixed delays) reaching every row up to and including the top ledge, plus isolated checks confirming the shallow-crumble timing and the fall-search's "usually closer, not always closest" distribution both behave as designed. **Tuning pass, same day, first real playtest feedback ("it is almost decaying too fast though... it is confusing why i keep dropping" / "it seems like there is a clean straight up line on the left side")**: caught and fixed a real layout bug -- the per-row candidate x-offsets were coming from one fixed array of slots, so "the left hold" landed at nearly the exact same world x on every row, forming a real, repeatable, reliable column straight up one side (worse, the "guarantee a survivable option" fallback always defaulted to that same slot too, making it extra safe). Each row now gets its own random spread and lane shift, and the safe-fallback lands on a random candidate in the row instead of always the same one -- no more straight line. Also loosened strong/weak grip windows back up a bit (1000/680 -> 1150/800) and shallow's timer slightly (470 -> 530), and gave shallow a genuinely different decay visual from weak -- weak keeps the blue spiderweb crack, shallow now shows an immediate grey-white "caving in" look with a spreading shadow, present from the moment you grab it rather than growing from a delay -- so a drop reads as a specific, recognizable cause instead of one generic unexplained thing. Confirmed for Sam directly: yes, aiming is literally holding Left/Right (in addition to Up to release) during the hop, same as asked. **Unrelated bug fix, same day, reported mid-thread ("on the icypool, when i am not in it yet but walking to it and pressing rightarrow, sometimes it just spazzes out keeping me at the edge of it")**: root cause was a one-frame-stale flag -- the game checks "am I on the watery ice" once per frame, but that check runs AFTER the walk-input code that same frame, so right at the pool's edge the walk code was always reacting to LAST frame's answer, not the current one; combined with the pool's own gentle current occasionally nudging the player back across the boundary, this could trap them oscillating right at the edge. Fixed by recomputing the zone check fresh, in the walk-input code itself, off the player's current position, instead of trusting the stale flag -- verified with a real-keyboard test holding Right for 4 straight seconds starting just outside the zone: zero direction reversals, clean progress through. **Shelf removal, same day ("can we remove the 'shelves' on the ice wall, just having the cracks and holds visible pls")**: the solid ice-ledge/shelf shapes sticking out of the wall at each hold (and the wedge bridging them to the wall) are gone entirely -- holds are now just a small marker plus the crack texture, which was always the part actually carrying the gameplay information. **Final visual pass, same day ("make the simple markings look better not just like ovals. make it look like holds int he ice!! and the rest of the ice wall better too like more realistic like how i have asked about th e other hanging ice thing rn that has the icicles on it")**: the hold marker (which had temporarily become a plain flat oval once the shelf was removed) is now a small carved notch -- a dark shadow pool sunk into the ice first, an irregular faceted lip instead of a smooth ellipse, plus one lit facet and one shadow facet layered on top, so it reads as a real chipped pocket in the ice with actual dimension rather than a flat colored dot. The wall itself now also gets a scatter of large soft angular ice facets (alternating faintly lighter/darker, clipped to the wall's own silhouette) borrowing the same lit/shadow-facet principle already established by the rainbow icicles elsewhere in winter, instead of being one flat gradient fill. Verified via screenshot (holds read as real icy chips, not ovals; wall shows genuine faceted texture) and a re-run steering test confirming climb mechanics are unaffected by the purely visual change. **Texture + top-ledge pass, same day ("key this better. but make ice a lil better still. more texture, some parts like stifkcing out" / "and chnage the top a lot it is the exact same as the rock pool climb top rn")**: two more real gaps closed. First, more texture and real relief on the wall itself -- the large facet scatter went from 22 to 34, plus a new finer layer of ~70 small frost-grain speckle flecks so the surface doesn't read as smooth between the big facets, and, the actual ask, a scatter of real 3D ice-chunk outcroppings that physically break the wall's own silhouette instead of staying flush and clipped to it -- each one anchored at the wall's edge and pushed out past it, with its own lit facet, shadow facet, and a soft contact shadow where it meets the wall, so it reads as genuinely projecting outward rather than more flat paint. Second, and separately flagged as a real problem ("the exact same as the rock pool climb top rn") -- the top landing ledge was, quite literally, `drawForestRockLedge` with the fill colors swapped to blue: same two smooth ellipses plus a highlight ellipse, no wonder it looked identical. Rebuilt from the ground up as a real jagged ice shelf: a broken angular front face instead of a rounded rim, an irregular faceted top surface (not a smooth oval) with its own small facet/crack texture clipped to it, a soft snow-cap highlight, and a handful of small icicles hanging off the front lip using the same tapering faceted-shard language as the wall holds and the rainbow icicles.
